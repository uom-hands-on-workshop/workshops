## Activity 2

### Domain Challenge: Evaluate Assessment Risk in Your Discipline

This activity invites you to explore how generative AI can be applied to **authentic assessment tasks in your domain**—and how it might challenge academic integrity, assessment design, and our assumptions about "safe" tasks.

We’ll use this shared Padlet board to collaborate and upload materials: [https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp](https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp)

---

#### Step 1: Choose or Refine a Task (2 mins)

**Objective**: Select or come up with a small assessment task or challenge from your discipline that can be shared publicly (just identify one for now - that's all).

**Instructions**:

- Choose an **example of assessment item or task** from your domain (ideally something short or modular) that is similar to your current assessment approach (but that can be shared with peers - nothing confidential).
- This should be something you can **judge the quality of in about 5 minutes**.
- It could be something you're **worried AI might complete too easily**, or something you believe is more resilient.
- Upload your assessment/task to the Padlet: [https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp](https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp)

**Examples**:
> *In my domain (Psychology), I selected a short written reflection task where students analyse a case study using psychological theories. Here is the task: ...*
>*Design a unity gain 2nd order active high-pass filter with a cut-off frequency of 3.4kHz, a quality factor of 1. Use real-world (idealised) components in your design. Justify any design choices you have made and provide a diagram of the circuit.*

Other example tasks:
- Short written reflections or discussion board posts
- Essay introductions
- Lab report sections
- Code snippets with documentation
- Mathematical proof setups
- Clinical or policy case evaluations
- Visual design briefs or diagrams
- Peer feedback responses
- Short video or audio scripts
- A design problem (software, infrastructure, architecture, ...)
- Problems that involve the creation of diagrams, sketchs or drawings

---

#### Step 2: Reflect on the Task (2 mins)

**Objective**: Critically reflect on the vulnerability and importance of your task.

**Instructions**:
- Ask yourself:
  - What aspects of this task might be at risk due to generative AI?
  - What parts do you feel are more protected or "safe"? Why?
  - How important is the assessed skill, knowledge, or competency in this task?
- Make some quick notes for yourself—we’ll come back to this reflection in Step 5.

---

#### Step 3: Attempt the Task Using genAI (10 mins)

**Objective**: Experience how genAI might complete a given task.

**Instructions**:

1. Find a partner in the workshop.
2. **Swap tasks** via the Padlet.
3. [working independently] Use any details or resources shared by your partner to attempt their task.
4. [working independently] Use **generative AI** tools to complete the task as if you were a **desperate student trying to avoid a fail**.

##### Tips for Using AI Tools in Step 3:

- **Start simple**: Paste the task directly into ChatGPT or your chosen tool.
- **Iterate**: If the output is poor, tell the tool what went wrong and ask it to fix it. Iterate multiple times.
- **Give feedback**: If the AI output looks wrong let it know! LLM's can often be quite responsive to direct and specific feedback or instructions.
- **Try personas**: Frame the task with a role prompt, e.g. *“You are an expert in structural analysis writing for undergraduate students...”*
- **Experiment with tools**: Try ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity, NotebookLM etc. 
    - **ChatGPT**: [https://chatgpt.com/?model=gpt-4o/](https://chatgpt.com/?model=gpt-4o/) *(free with account)*
    - **Claude**: [https://claude.ai/](https://claude.ai/) *(free with account)*
    - **Gemini**: [https://gemini.google.com/](https://gemini.google.com/)
    - **Perplexity**: [https://www.perplexity.ai/](https://www.perplexity.ai/)
    - **NotebookLM**: [https://notebooklm.google.com/](https://notebooklm.google.com/)
    - **SparkAI**: [https://spark.unimelb.edu.au/](https://spark.unimelb.edu.au/)
- **Create pipelines**: Use multiple tools. One might write, another might refine, another might check for tone or accuracy.
- **Check assumptions**: Some tools hallucinate—watch for confident nonsense.
- **Focus on the product**: Would this submission pass? Would it fool you?

---

#### Step 4: Submit Your Response (1 min)

**Objective**: Share your generated submission and rate your familiarity.

**Instructions**:

- Upload your AI-generated solution to the Padlet: [https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp](https://go.unimelb.edu.au/8vrp)
- In the comments section, **rate yourself**:
  - Your **expertise in the task domain** (1 = no familiarity, 5 = expert)
  - Your **expertise with genAI tools** (1 = beginner, 5 = expert)

---

#### Step 5: Share and Discuss (20 mins)

**Objective**: Evaluate what happened and what it means for your teaching.

**Instructions**:

- Take turns presenting your solution to your partner (~10 mins each - please be mindful of time :D).
- Discuss:
  - The **quality** of the AI-generated work.
  - The **steps you took** to generate it.
  - What could be improved? What surprised you?
  - What are the **implications for the original assessment**?
  - Refer back to your reflection in Step 2 — were your assumptions correct?
